Open data / Clear provenance

Know the source.

Merchant data comes from people and projects doing the work to make transaction descriptions useful. Here are the sources Ultrafinance supports, the credit they deserve, and how we use their data.

These are supported import and evaluation sources. The catalog contains the snapshots explicitly imported into it, plus manual records and corrections; it is not a live mirror of every dataset below. Explore the current catalog.

Merchant catalog sources

These datasets supply merchant identities and evidence for matching transaction descriptions.

Merchant Studio ↗

Data: CC BY 4.0

Enrichment from Merchant Studio by Jonathan Taveras

Merchant names, statement aliases, website information, and country hints. Its sample descriptors also support source consistency evaluations.

Our adapter normalizes website URLs, preserves source evidence, and assigns local merchant IDs. Country hints are evidence of known markets, not exhaustive coverage. Source confidence values are not our measured accuracy.

Open Enrichment ↗

CSV data: CC0 1.0

Open Enrichment by Steve Harrison

Merchant names, websites, transaction examples, and matching patterns from global and country-specific CSV datasets. Separately imported outlet records can provide location evidence.

Child places are excluded from the brand catalog. Country-specific dataset regions provide market evidence; global scope does not establish a merchant’s operating countries. Merchant icons are not imported and are excluded from the dataset license.

Foursquare OS Places ↗

Apache 2.0

Foursquare OS Places by Foursquare Labs, Inc.

Token-authenticated downloads through the Places Portal Iceberg catalog and merchant imports from filtered place exports, including business names, websites, categories, and country evidence. Reviewed brand mappings combine related places into one merchant while preserving the contributing Foursquare place IDs.

Unmapped same-name businesses remain separate. Imported names remain candidate evidence; this adapter creates no transaction aliases, matching patterns, logos, or location records. It supplies business knowledge rather than transaction accuracy labels.

Transaction evaluation datasets

These adapters prepare transaction examples for evaluation. Category-only and unlabeled examples need merchant labels before they can measure merchant recognition. Synthetic reference catalogs are intended for isolated testing.

DoDataThings ↗

MIT

DoDataThings us-bank-transaction-categories-v2

Synthetic descriptions modeled on US bank statement formats, with spending category labels. Our adapter retains those categories and marks the examples as synthetic; category labels do not establish merchant identity.

MoneyData / MoneyVis ↗

CC BY 4.0

MoneyData / MoneyVis by Robert Laramee and collaborators

Bank transaction descriptions and amounts for evaluation. Our adapter retains transaction type and deliberately discards account, sort code, and balance columns. Merchant identities remain unlabeled until explicitly annotated.

BusinessTransactions ↗

CC BY 4.0 · upstream Apache 2.0

HighkeyPrxneeth; business names derived from Foursquare OS Places

Generated descriptions paired with business names, for reviewed synthetic retrieval tests. Names-only reference records use normalized-name identifiers, not place IDs. Generated descriptions never become aliases or merchant evidence, and generated geography and store numbers never become outlet facts. Results measure source consistency rather than accuracy on real transactions.

How source data becomes a result

Imports preserve source identities, external IDs, attribution, licenses, and snapshot versions. Dataset bundles also record input fingerprints and adapter versions. Refreshing an import preserves local merchant IDs and manual corrections. Records from different sources are linked explicitly rather than automatically merged by name.

Manually verified names and aliases can resolve unique exact matches locally. Imported aliases remain unverified; ambiguous and fuzzy candidates are evaluated by Jev, our AI matching provider. Jev chooses among catalog candidates rather than supplying a separate merchant dataset. Enrichment results derived from source-linked merchants include attribution information.

Coverage depends on the imported data and corrections. A missing market does not mean a merchant does not operate there. Source-derived evaluations measure consistency with a dataset, not accuracy on unseen real transactions.

For import details and reproducible evaluations, see the project documentation. To report incorrect data or suggest a source, open an issue.
